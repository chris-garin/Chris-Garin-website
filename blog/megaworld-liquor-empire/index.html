Megaworld’s Secret Liquor Empire: The Emperador Story

Chris Garin • November 16, 2026 • 5 min read

The short answer

Megaworld is the crown jewel of Andrew Tan’s Alliance Global Group, but the same conglomerate also owns Emperador, the world’s biggest brandy maker. Tan launched Emperador Brandy in 1990 to sidestep San Miguel’s beer, Ginebra’s gin, and Tanduay’s rum, selling affordable brandy that felt classy instead. By 2006 it was the world’s best-selling brandy by volume, and it went on to buy Scotland’s Whyte & Mackay and Spain’s Fundador. Its steady, recession-proof cash helps fund the property side.

Megaworld is kind of like the crown jewel of Andrew Tan’s empire, Alliance Global Group. But did you know the same group also built one of the biggest liquor empires on the entire planet?

How did a real estate company end up in liquor?

In the 1980s, Andrew Tan was an appliance merchant. After the 1986 EDSA Revolution, he spotted a massive opening in property, so in 1989 he started Megaworld. Condos first, then entire townships.

Megaworld handles the real estate, but it sits inside Alliance Global Group (AGI), a diversified holding company. And AGI’s aggressive push into liquor wasn’t random. It was a deliberate play to capture how Filipinos actually spend, and to insulate the conglomerate from economic cycles.

Why would a property developer want a liquor business?

Real estate makes a lot of money, but it requires a lot of cash. You spend billions to build the property, wait years for returns, and if the economy slows down, people stop buying condos. Development runs on massive capital outlays, long gestation periods, and credit cycles.

Liquor is the opposite. Make a bottle today, sell it today, get paid today. Mass-market spirits generate steady, high-margin, recurring cash flow. And it’s one of the few truly recession-proof businesses. When times are good, people drink to celebrate. When times are bad, people drink to forget.

So a liquor arm wasn’t a distraction from Megaworld. It was the twin engine: reliable cash when the property market cools, funding for aggressive land acquisitions when it heats up.

Why brandy, and not beer, gin, or rum?

If you wanted into Philippine liquor back then, the obvious lanes were taken. San Miguel owned beer. Ginebra owned gin. Tanduay owned rum. Fighting any of them head-on meant a brutal, low-margin price war against entrenched giants.

Gin and rum had another problem: they were seen as the cheap stuff, tied to low-tier drinking sessions. Tan needed something different. Something that felt classy, but that everyone could afford.

Then he remembered something. Growing up poor, his father once splurged on a single bottle of brandy to celebrate Andrew’s college graduation. To him, brandy meant one thing: hard-earned success. Brandy carried that European connotation of prestige and class. And the best part? No existing conglomerate was selling it.

So in 1990, he launched Emperador Brandy. It tasted the way Filipinos liked, smooth and tuned to the local palate. It was cheap enough for anyone. But it looked expensive, like something from Europe. It was marketed not as a cheap drunk, but as accessible luxury. He didn’t fight the giants. He walked around them, and created a lane where consumers could trade up psychologically without paying import prices.

How big did Emperador actually get?

It worked, massively. Emperador officially became the world’s best-selling brandy by volume in 2006, just 16 years after it launched.

Then it went shopping abroad. In 2014, Emperador made a landmark $720 million acquisition of Whyte & Mackay, the fifth-largest Scotch whisky producer, instantly gaining global distribution networks, historic Scottish brands, and a foothold in the lucrative international whisky segment.

And then came the ultimate flex. In 2015, Emperador bought Fundador, Spain’s oldest and largest brandy house. Almost 300 years old, complete with centuries-old cellars and vineyards. The brand behind Spain’s first-ever bottled brandy, now owned by a Filipino company.

Today, Emperador is a juggernaut: the largest liquor company in the Philippines, the world’s biggest brandy maker, pulling in around ₱55 to 60 billion in revenue a year with products in over 100 countries.

Is Emperador bigger than Megaworld?

It’s a natural question given Emperador’s global footprint, but no. Megaworld is still the financial heavyweight within AGI, typically contributing the lion’s share of the group’s core revenues and net income through residential sales, office leasing, and lifestyle malls. Emperador runs a close race as the global cash and volume titan.

Together they form a symbiotic loop: Emperador provides steady, recession-proof cash flow, while Megaworld locks it down into permanent, appreciating land banks. And the ecosystem goes further. AGI also spans quick-service restaurants through McDonald’s Philippines, plus leisure and gaming, while Megaworld’s malls, hotels, and townships provide prime commercial footprints for nightlife, dining, and retail distribution.

The lesson

Tan found the one liquor category that wasn’t fiercely contested and built a new market segment around an emotional insight: Filipinos wanted an affordable drink that felt like success. He didn’t just capture market share. He created a lane, dominated it at home, then used it to swallow global names like Whyte & Mackay and Fundador whole.

Emperador and Megaworld FAQ

Who owns Emperador Brandy?

Emperador Inc. is part of Alliance Global Group (AGI), the conglomerate of billionaire Andrew Tan, the same group that controls Megaworld. Tan launched Emperador Brandy in 1990, and it grew into the largest liquor company in the Philippines and the world’s biggest brandy maker.

Is Emperador bigger than Megaworld?

No. Megaworld is still the financial heavyweight of Alliance Global Group, typically contributing the biggest share of core revenues and net income. Emperador is the group’s global cash and volume titan, generating tens of billions of pesos a year across more than 100 countries.

Why did Andrew Tan choose brandy?

San Miguel owned beer, Ginebra owned gin, and Tanduay owned rum, so fighting them meant a brutal price war. Gin and rum were also seen as the cheap stuff. Brandy carried a classy European image, no conglomerate was selling it, and for Tan it was personal: his father once splurged on a bottle of brandy to celebrate his college graduation.

What foreign brands does Emperador own?

In 2014, Emperador acquired Whyte & Mackay, the fifth-largest Scotch whisky producer, for $720 million. In 2015, it bought Fundador, Spain’s oldest and largest brandy house, almost 300 years old and the maker of Spain’s first bottled brandy.
