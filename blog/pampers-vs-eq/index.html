The Fall of Pampers in the Philippines: How EQ Won

Chris Garin • November 16, 2026 • 4 min read

The short answer

Pampers was once so dominant that Filipinos called every diaper “Pampers.” But its premium pricing turned a recurring product into a never-ending bill for families. In 1989, an Ilonggo electrical-supplies trader named Samuel Po, frustrated as a new father himself, started importing better, cheaper diapers and built EQ: Economy and Quality. By 2012, EQ had overtaken P&G in diapers nationwide. Then Shopee and Lazada flooded the market with cheap Asian brands, and in November 2025, P&G pulled Pampers out of the Philippines entirely.

This is the story of how Pampers collapsed in the Philippines, at the hands of a homegrown brand: EQ.

How dominant was Pampers in the Philippines?

Pampers got so big, it achieved every marketer’s ultimate dream. Filipinos stopped saying diaper. Every diaper was just “Pampers,” even when it was a different brand.

When P&G brought Pampers here, their bet was simple: Filipino parents won’t risk anything less for their baby, so they’ll always pay more for a global brand. And for a while, that worked.

They were also sitting on one of the best business models out there. Diapers are recurring. One baby goes through thousands of them before potty training, and every year there’s a fresh batch of new parents.

But what was a dream for P&G was a never-ending bill for Filipino families. A pack this week. Another next week. For two, three years straight. Pampers didn’t know it yet, but that pain would create the brand that took it down.

Who started EQ diapers?

In 1989, an Ilonggo in Manila named Samuel Po and his wife had their first child. They needed diapers, and they needed them now. But diapers were hard to find. He even drove to nearby cities, only to find stocks had run out. And when he finally did find them, his reaction was: wait, this is it? This is the quality? At this price?

His next thought: if I’m struggling with this, how many other Filipino parents are too? Maybe this wasn’t just a problem. Maybe it was an opportunity.

Po wasn’t a diaper guy. He sold electrical supplies. But he asked his Taiwan supplier for diaper samples, and it turned out they were way better than what was sold here. He hesitated. Should he order more and sell them? What if nobody buys? Eventually he took the risk. The supplier’s minimum order was two full containers. He got them into a leading supermarket chain, they sold like hotcakes, and just like that, he was in the diaper distribution business.

What made EQ different from Pampers?

The big idea came when Po was in Malaysia and saw a “basic diaper.” A plain transparent bag with just a sticker. No fancy packaging. Every peso went into the diaper itself.

He understood something P&G’s premium bet missed: Filipinos didn’t need the most advanced diaper in the world. Just one that didn’t leak, didn’t cause rashes, and didn’t wreck the budget. That became EQ: Economy and Quality.

And it worked. By 2012, EQ had overtaken P&G in diapers nationwide.

Why did Pampers leave the Philippines?

Then came the 2020s. Shopee and Lazada opened the floodgates to cheaper diaper brands from all over Asia. Pampers wasn’t fighting one EQ anymore. It was fighting a whole wave.

In November 2025, P&G pulled Pampers out of the Philippines. The fall of the giant was complete.

The brand that was once the generic word for diaper lost to a homegrown player who felt the customer’s pain firsthand, priced for the Filipino budget, and put every peso into the product instead of the packaging.
