SM’s Pickleball Obsession Isn’t About Pickleball

Chris Garin • November 16, 2026 • 6 min read

The short answer

SM has built over 100 pickleball courts across 32 malls, but the sport is not the business. The courts give you a reason to show up that Shopee and Lazada can never ship: a real-life experience. Players come weekly, stay to eat and shop, and book everything through the SM Malls Online app, where SM’s sports community now has over 130,000 members. The experience is the bait. The mall is the business.

SM just built over 100 pickleball courts across 32 malls. But it was never about the sport. SM finally found the one thing Shopee and Lazada can never deliver.

I made a short video about this too, if you’d rather watch the quick version first.

How fast did SM build all these courts?

Fast. In May 2025, SM had just 12 courts in three malls. By mid 2026, a Philstar report counted 86 courts across 29 malls. Today, SM says it has 102 courts across 32 malls. That’s the biggest mall-based pickleball network in the country, and it includes purpose-built venues like the six-court facility at Four E-Com Center.

So why build all of that if the courts don’t need to make money? SM even holds free open play sessions. Here’s my read: SM isn’t really in the pickleball business. It’s fighting Shopee.

Why is SM fighting e-commerce with sports?

Think about why you used to go to the mall. Sure, to buy things. But also for the aircon. To walk around. To eat, catch a movie, hang out. For decades, the mall was the Filipino third place. Not home, not work, but the place you went to just be.

But today, you don’t need to go to the mall to buy anything. You open an app, and it shows up at your door. Every time you shop online instead, that’s one less reason to visit the mall. So SM needs reasons for you to show up that can’t be shipped to your house.

The timing matters too. We were stuck at home with our phones for years during the pandemic. We did everything online, and it worked, but it was suffocating. Then the lockdowns ended, and what did we do? Revenge travel. Concerts. Fun runs. Everyone rushed back to the one thing we’d been missing: real experiences. You can get almost anything delivered now, but you can’t get an experience delivered. You have to show up for it. And for a mall, getting you to show up is everything.

Why fitness, and why pickleball specifically?

Because fitness became the new flex. There’s a concept called social currency. Jonah Berger, a marketing professor at Wharton, explains it simply: we share things that make us look good. Sure, people still flex the new bag and the new phone. But for that kind of flex, money is the barrier.

Fitness changed that. It’s the great equalizer. Running is free. Anyone can lace up and go for a run, anywhere. And you can’t buy a 10K finish or your Strava stats. You have to work for them for months. For this kind of flex, the barrier isn’t money. It’s consistency. It’s effort. That hits on a different level, because it’s personal. It says something about who you are, not just what you can afford. And it’s not just Gen Z anymore. Your titos and titas are posting their runs too.

Then came the pickleball boom, currently the fastest-growing sport in the Philippines. It’s one of the least intimidating sports to start. You can learn the ropes in a few minutes, you get to play with your friends, and it’s made for all age groups. SM saw all this, but it didn’t just see a trend. It realized it had found its greatest defense against Shopee and Lazada: delivering real-life experiences.

What is SM Active Hub?

SM Active Hub is SM’s own sports community, launched in March 2025 by SM President Steven Tan, who called it the country’s largest sports playground. It started with pickleball and running, plus tournaments, fun runs, training clinics, and wellness workshops. Launching it basically said the quiet part out loud: malls shouldn’t just be places to shop. They should be places to move and connect.

The running side is bigger than people think. SM now has 24 malls with dedicated running paths, 45 running clubs, and 55 running events mounted so far. The SM2SM Run in Cebu drew 15,500 runners in its 14th year, and the Women’s Run at MOA drew 16,500.

On the pickleball side, SM has staged over 100 tournaments, signed brand partnerships like the SM Pickleball Super Series with Toby’s Sports, and flown in pro athletes like James Ignatowich and MacKonner Dy to build sports credibility. And the events are engineered to be posted: the Pink Paddle Club at SM City East Ortigas, glow-in-the-dark Night Glow pickleball at SM City Cabanatuan, partnerships with creator collectives like The Friday Crew. Every post from a mallgoer is free marketing.

How do free courts make SM money?

The courts do two things for SM. First, they get you through the door. SM now has a way to bring you to the mall without holding a sale or a big event. You go there regularly for a game of pickleball or a run.

Second, they keep you there longer. Nobody plays two hours of pickleball and goes straight home. You eat. You grab a coffee. You walk around. And maybe you buy those shoes you passed on the way in.

Frequency is the real prize. A movie is a once-a-month trip. But a Tuesday pickleball game? A Sunday run club? That’s every week. That’s a habit. And ever wonder why so many fun runs start early on a Sunday? My guess is that’s when a mall is usually dead. A run fills the parking lot at 5 a.m. with thousands of people who’ll be hungry right when the food court opens. The runs are also mostly sponsor-funded. The Doraemon Run alone had Katinko, Salonpas, Nutrifizz, Fitbar, Quaker, and others as sponsors. SM provides the venue and gets the crowd, while brands pay for most of the show.

A pickleball court with no community has an expiration date. SM doesn’t have that problem, because it doesn’t need the court to make money. The court is the bait. The mall is the business.

What’s the smartest part of the strategy?

The app. You discover events, book courts, and unlock rewards through the SM Malls Online app, and SM’s sports community now has over 130,000 members. That’s 130,000 people SM can reach directly. No ads. No algorithm. Just a notification on your phone the next time there’s a game, a race, or a sale.

So SM is building a moat with three layers. Location, because there’s probably an SM near you. Community, because your group plays there. And data, because SM learns who its most active customers are.

An online store can deliver your shoes. But it can’t give you a game on a Tuesday night with your friends. That’s how SM is fighting Shopee. Not by selling you more things. By giving you more reasons to show up.

SM pickleball FAQ

How many pickleball courts does SM have?

SM says it now has 102 courts across 32 malls, the biggest mall-based pickleball network in the Philippines. The growth has been rapid: 12 courts in three malls in May 2025, 86 courts across 29 malls by mid 2026, and over 100 today, including a purpose-built six-court venue at Four E-Com Center.

What is SM Active Hub?

SM Active Hub is SM’s sports community, launched in March 2025 by SM President Steven Tan as the country’s largest sports playground. It covers pickleball courts, dedicated running paths in 24 malls, 45 running clubs, 55 running events so far, plus tournaments, clinics, and wellness workshops.

Why is SM investing so heavily in pickleball and fun runs?

Because experiences are the one thing e-commerce can’t deliver. Online shopping removes reasons to visit a mall, so SM is monetizing things that require you to show up. Sports also builds weekly habits: a movie is a once-a-month trip, but a Tuesday pickleball game or a Sunday run club happens every week, and players stay to eat and shop.

Do SM’s pickleball courts make money?

The courts don’t need to. SM even holds free open play sessions. The real returns are foot traffic, longer mall visits, sponsor-funded events, and the SM Malls Online app, where the Active Hub community has over 130,000 members SM can reach directly with offers, events, and sales.
